Underwater cleaning should have a specific purpose. The goal might be to reveal a maker's mark, inspect a crack, or prepare a small area for measurement. It should not automatically mean making the entire cannon look new.

## Define the goal

Write the cleaning goal in one sentence before selecting a tool. A narrow goal keeps the work focused and helps prevent accidental removal of stable surface material.

Examples of useful goals include:

- Expose the edge of an inscription for photography.
- Remove loose growth from one measurement point.
- Check whether a visible line is a crack or attached marine material.

## Begin with the least force

Start with water movement or a soft hand tool approved by the project conservator. Work slowly in a small test area, then stop and inspect the result. Never assume that a tool is safe merely because its bristles or edge feel soft above water.

## Record the test patch

Photograph the patch before, during, and after cleaning. Record the tool, duration, and result. If the cleaned area reveals a fragile layer, staining, or unexpected movement, stop and ask for a conservation review.

## Know when to stop

A successful cleaning session can end with only a few square centimeters completed. The best stopping point is the moment the stated goal has been met without creating a new conservation problem.
