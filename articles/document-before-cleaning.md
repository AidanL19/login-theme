Before any brush touches the cannon, record its condition. A short survey gives the team a shared starting point and makes later changes easier to explain.

## Build a visual record

Photograph the cannon from the same repeatable angles: both sides, the muzzle, the cascabel, and each mounting point. Add a scale marker to close photographs so cracks, shells, and corrosion can be measured later.

Use a simple naming pattern for the image files, such as `site-object-angle-date`. Consistent names are easier to sort than a folder full of camera-generated numbers.

## Note the environment

Write down water depth, visibility, current strength, and the visible types of marine growth. These conditions affect both the cleaning plan and the quality of the photographs.

> A survey is not busywork. It is the evidence that lets a conservation team compare before and after conditions.

## Make a basic sketch

The sketch does not need to be artistic. Mark the cannon's orientation, nearby objects, damaged areas, and any surface that should not be disturbed. Number those areas so the same labels can be used in photographs and notes.

Finish the survey by asking one question: **Can another person understand the cannon's current condition without entering the water?** If the answer is yes, the record is ready.
